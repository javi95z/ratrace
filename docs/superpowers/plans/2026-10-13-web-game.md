## Why steps 9 and 10 wait

Step 9 (unlocks, achievements, collection, records, share card) stores its data through the `PlayerStore` and shows it on screens that this step creates. Step 10 (guided first life, desktop polish, accessibility pass) polishes screens that do not exist yet. Neither can be finished before this step, so this step goes first.

## What this adds

`apps/web`: the game in a browser, phone first. A whole life can be played, closed, reopened and finished, in English or Spanish, with the final sprites.

Not in this step: achievements, the collection, records, the share card (step 9); the guided first life and the accessibility audit (step 10); anything online (steps 11 to 15).

## Decisions

### The engine view (roadmap step 4)

- `GameView` is a `GameState` without `seed`, without `rng`, and with a timeline that stops at the current round. The seed gives away every future price and the saved random stream gives away every future dice roll, so neither may reach a client in ranked play.
- `viewOf(state)` makes one. `previewRound`, `previewCard` and the selectors accept a view, so the client never needs the full state to show a number. `applyCards` and `applyRound` need the full state and stay where the full state lives.
- The invariant check splits in two: everything that can be checked on a view, and the two checks that need the full state (timeline length, saved random stream).

### Two interfaces, and nothing else, between screens and game

Exactly as the online design asks:

- `GameSession`: start a game, read the current view, answer the cards, play a round. `LocalSession` runs the engine in the browser and keeps the full state to itself.
- `PlayerStore`: the game in progress, finished runs, and the profile (settings now; unlocks, achievements and collection in step 9). `LocalStore` keeps them in IndexedDB.

No screen imports the engine's `applyCards` or `applyRound`, and no screen touches IndexedDB. A test enforces both.

### Saved games

- A save is: format number, game id (a UUID made on the device), engine version, content version, life id, seed, the log of answers and orders for every round played, and the latest full state. It is written after the cards are answered and after every round.
- Loading checks the versions and runs the engine's invariant check on the stored state. If the state is damaged, the log is replayed from the seed; if that fails too, the save is dropped and the player is offered a new life. The profile is never touched by a bad save.
- A finished life is written to `runs` with its score, net worth, escape round and the net worth at every round, so step 9 has its history from day one.

### Text

- Interface text lives in `apps/web/messages/en.json` and `es.json`. A test fails if the two files do not have the same keys.
- Game text (cards, items, careers, headlines, refusal reasons, ledger lines) comes from `@ratrace/content`, which already holds it in both languages with its own lint test.
- Money uses `Intl.NumberFormat` with `currency: 'USD'` and `currencyDisplay: 'narrowSymbol'`, as spec 8.6 says.

### Look

The look is the mock-up approved on 2026-10-12, the "fine" pixel size on the night palette:

- Navy screen `#141b33`, panels `#1f2a4d`, ink `#f4f1e4`, lines `#0b1022`, gold `#f2c14e` for the one primary action on a screen, cream `#f4f1e4` for cards and item tiles. Only the 31 interface colours of spec section 6 are used.
- Pixelify Sans for titles and numbers, Inter for body text. Both are self-hosted through `next/font`, so no request leaves the page for a font.
- Chrome is drawn in CSS: 2px stepped pixel borders, no rounded corners, no blur, no gradients except the sky.
- Sprites render at whole multiples only, with `image-rendering: pixelated`: one art pixel is one CSS pixel on a phone and two on a desktop.
- A sprite that is missing shows a drawn placeholder and logs once. The game is fully playable without art.

### Screens in this step

| Screen | Content |
|---|---|
| Home | Continue the life in progress, new life, language, reduced motion |
| New life | The six lives with perk, handicap and portrait. A locked life shows its goal |
| HUD | Skyline of the city, portrait at the right age, age, cash, yearly cashflow, status, the rat-race bar |
| News | Three headlines, the gauges, and which of last round's headlines came true |
| Cards | One card at a time: art, text, each option with what it costs and does, its odds, and whether it is open. Swipe or tap |
| Finance | Tabs: Portfolio, Market, Property, Debt, Lifestyle, Shop, Taxes. A basket of this round's orders, each removable. The play button always shows cash after the round |
| Resolve | The ledger rolls up line by line; promotions, moves, escape and bankruptcy are called out; the rat-race bar moves |
| Profile | Job, skills, family, insurance, focus, debts, homes, things owned |
| End of life | Net-worth graph, score breakdown, play again |

Spec section 5 lists six finance tabs. Shopping for status items is a seventh, because inside "Lifestyle" it buried 34 items under the lifestyle controls on a phone.

Interaction rules of spec section 5 that hold from the first commit: primary actions in the bottom half; buying and selling in a bottom sheet with a slider and 25%, 50% and 100% chips; every swipe has a button; tap targets are at least 44px; reduced motion turns off roll-ups and particles. At 1024px and wider the same components sit in three columns.
